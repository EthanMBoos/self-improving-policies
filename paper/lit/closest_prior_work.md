# Closest prior work: grader FP vs FN in robot self-improvement (checked 2026-11-21)

Our claim: in filtered-imitation self-improvement (pi0.5, LIBERO), grader FPs hurt and FNs cost little; Robometer-4B's effect equals random errors at its FP/FN rates.
Sources: arXiv abstracts. I also read the full text (PDF) of RoboRef, SIEFM, pi*0.6, Tian et al., PU-BC and Hu et al. App. C.

## Requested papers (all exist)
- **Tian, Wu, Bajcsy. "Position: Good Embodied Reward Models Need Bad Behavior Data." arXiv 2606.01036, ICML 2026 (position track, spotlight).** Shows 3 SOTA embodied RMs over-reward bad behavior, and that bad-behavior data cuts FPs. Measures RM-vs-human agreement only. No policy is trained. *Diff:* motivates "FPs are costly" but never measures the policy effect.
- **Yao et al. "Multimodal Reward Hacking in Reinforcement Learning." arXiv 2607.09492 (preprint).** RL (GRPO/RLOO/DAPO) on MLLMs for VQA. Proposes the NRFR metric and finds 48.1% hacking with outcome-only rewards. *Diff:* language/VQA, not robots. Varies reward design, not FP/FN rates.
- **Navasardyan, Danielyan, Davtyan. "FailBench: How Reliable are VLMs at Judging Robot Task Success?" arXiv 2609.03611 (preprint).** 2,197 attempts, 13 detectors, best balanced accuracy 0.77, bias toward predicting success. Measures detector accuracy only. *Diff:* gives evidence that real graders lean FP. We measure what that does to the policy.
- **Karageorgis, Barcellona, Aljundi, Gavves, Gumbsch. "RoboRef: A Foundation Reward Model Built for Robot RL, Not Offline Metrics." NeurIPS 2026 Workshop (RoboPAD). No arXiv; PDF at roboref-reward.github.io.** Fine-tunes Robometer-4B on failures, with an asymmetric loss that penalizes over-prediction. Online RL (SAC on ManiSkill, IBRL on MetaWorld, RLPD on a real Franka). Offline accuracy vs RL success across 6 RMs: Kendall tau = -0.87. Policy success falls as the rate of over-scored failures rises (tau = 0.60). Base Robometer-4B gets 0 success. **Closest robot work.** They argue FP >> FN, but from correlations across models plus a loss ablation. They inject no controlled FP/FN errors. *Diff:* online RL with dense/sparse rewards, not filtered BC on a VLA. Their FP-vs-FN claim is observational. For FP >> FN they cite [16] below.
- **Afrin, Shihab. "Evaluator Ensembles Under Reward Hacking: Covariance Geometry and Finite-Search Guarantees." arXiv 2608.08002 (preprint).** Theory showing shared judge errors survive ensembling, with best-of-K bounds and Gaussian stress tests. *Diff:* LLM judges, no policy training, no FP/FN split.
- **Ghasemipour, Wahid, Tompson, Sanketi, Mordatch. "Self-Improving Embodied Foundation Models." arXiv 2509.15155, NeurIPS 2025.** RL self-improvement with a steps-to-go reward and a self-derived success detector. The paper calls the detector "very robust" but runs no error analysis (checked the full text). *Diff:* no label-error study.
- **Physical Intelligence. "pi*0.6: a VLA That Learns From Experience" (RECAP). arXiv 2511.14759 (preprint).** Advantage-conditioned offline RL on episode success labels, which humans provide. No label-error analysis (checked the full text). *Diff:* assumes clean labels.

## Other relevant work found
- **Gumbsch et al. "From Demonstrations to Rewards: Test-Time Prompt Optimization for VLM Reward Models" (Demo2Reward). arXiv 2606.00083, RLC 2026.** Prompt-tunes a VLM RM to cut FPs while keeping TPs, used for simulated robot RL. It asserts that FPs severely degrade policy learning. Its abstract describes no isolated FP-vs-FN ablation; I did not read the full PDF. *Diff:* RL, method paper. **Must check the full text before submission.**
- **Hu, Shridhar, Lu, Shah, Chiang, Tan, Xie. "What Matters in Orchestrating Robot Policies: A Systematic Study of Hierarchical VLA Agents." arXiv 2606.10267 (preprint), App. C.** Injects 10/30/50% FP or FN, independent per state, into a success detector. 10% is harmless; high FP is drastic; FN is moderate. **This is a controlled FP-vs-FN robot study with the same rates as ours.** But the detector is a runtime termination signal, not a training-data filter. *Diff:* inference-time orchestration, no policy training. Must cite.
- **Huang, Liu, Lipovetzky, Cohn. "The Dark Side of Rich Rewards: Understanding and Mitigating Noise in VLM Rewards." arXiv 2409.15922, PRL @ ICAPS 2025.** Controlled injection: dropping x% of true subgoal rewards (FN) vs adding small rewards to x% of irrelevant states (FP). FP hurts much more. Environments: Crafter, Montezuma, Minigrid. *Diff:* RL in game/grid worlds, not robot manipulation or filtered BC.
- **Egashira, Vero, Dekoninck, Dorner, Staab, Vechev. "Delay, Plateau, or Collapse: Evaluating the Impact of Systematic Verification Error on RLVR." arXiv 2605.02909, COLM 2026.** Controlled arithmetic RLVR. Systematic FNs act like random noise; systematic FPs cause plateaus or collapse. The error pattern matters, not just the rate. *Diff:* LLMs. **This directly bears on our "real grader = matched random" result.** Discuss it as a contrast: Robometer's FPs did not behave systematically in our setting.
- **Cai et al. "Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable yet Noisy Rewards under Imperfect Verifiers." arXiv 2510.00915.** Models the verifier as a noisy channel with separate FP/FN rates and proposes corrections. LLM RLVR. *Diff:* LLMs, correction method.
- **Wang et al. "Improving Behavioural Cloning with Positive Unlabeled Learning." CoRL 2023 (PMLR 229).** Filtered BC with a learned expert classifier. Reports classifier FP/FN counts but runs no controlled FP-vs-FN ablation (checked the PDF). *Diff:* mixed-quality offline data, not self-improvement.
- **Lee, Wagenmaker, Pertsch, Liang, Levine, Finn. "RoboReward." arXiv 2601.00675.** RM benchmark plus real-robot RL. Better RMs give better RL. No controlled noise injection. Also: Bedi, arXiv 2606.03134 (false-success detectability, no policy training).

## Verdict
"Has not been studied for robot policies" is **not defensible as written**. The asymmetry is already claimed for robot RL (RoboRef, Demo2Reward, Tian et al.). It has been tested with controlled injection in non-robot RL (Huang et al.; Egashira et al. for LLMs). It has been tested at our exact rates for a robot success detector at runtime (Hu et al.).

What remains novel, as far as this search found: **the first controlled study of grader FP vs FN on the *training data* of robot policy self-improvement by filtered imitation (rejection-sampling fine-tuning of a VLA), with a real VLM grader compared against random errors matched to its rates.** Suggested wording:

> "Prior work argues that false positives are the more harmful reward error in robot RL [RoboRef, Demo2Reward, Tian], shows it with controlled injection in non-robotic RL [Huang; Egashira], and for runtime success detection in hierarchical VLAs [Hu]. To our knowledge, no work has isolated FP and FN grader errors in the data used to fine-tune a robot policy on its own rollouts, or tested whether a real VLM grader's errors act like random errors at the same rates."

Frame the matched-random result against Egashira et al.: in their setting, systematic FPs were worse than random ones. Caveat: several papers are recent preprints. Searches were not exhaustive. Demo2Reward's full text should be checked.
