# Related work: noisy demos, asymmetric label noise, imperfect rewards/verifiers

Checked 2026-11-21 against arXiv/PMLR/venue pages unless marked.

## 1. Imitation from noisy / suboptimal demonstrations

- **Wu, Charoenphakdee, Bao, Tangkaratt, Sugiyama. "Imitation Learning from Imperfect Demonstration." ICML 2019 (PMLR 97).** Weighting demos by confidence (2IWIL, IC-GAIL) recovers good policies even when confidence labels exist for only a small share of the demos. *Relation:* our grader is a hard 0/1 confidence. We don't reweight; we measure what its errors do.
- **Sasaki, Yamashina. "Behavioral Cloning from Noisy Demonstrations." ICLR 2021.** Plain BC cannot reach the optimal policy when non-optimal demos are mixed in. Their fix reweights the BC loss with an earlier BC policy, which assumes optimal demos are the majority. *Relation:* our rho is the share of non-optimal data, so it says when that majority assumption fails.
- **Chen et al. (Lu, Rajeswaran, Lee, Grover, Laskin, Abbeel, Srinivas, Mordatch). "Decision Transformer." NeurIPS 2021.** Their "%BC" baseline runs BC on the top-X% of trajectories by return and is competitive. *Relation:* filtered self-imitation is %BC with a learned, error-prone filter in place of the true return.
- **Kumar, Hong, Singh, Levine. "When Should We Prefer Offline RL Over Behavioral Cloning?" ICLR 2022.** BC, including filtered BC, falls behind offline RL on noisy, sparse-reward, long-horizon data. *Relation:* this motivates keeping the kept set clean, since that is the regime where BC weakens.
- Optional, for robotics context: Peters & Schaal, "Reinforcement Learning by Reward-Weighted Regression for Operational Space Control," ICML 2007 (the RWR origin; cited from memory, not re-verified). Ha, Florence, Song, "Scaling Up and Distilling Down," CoRL 2023, which filters robot data by success with LLM-written success checks. Bousmalis et al., "RoboCat," TMLR 12/2023, a robot agent that improves itself by retraining on its own generated data.

## 2. Asymmetric / class-conditional label noise and PU learning

- **Natarajan, Dhillon, Ravikumar, Tewari. "Learning with Noisy Labels." NeurIPS 2013.** Class-conditional noise rates (rho_+1 ≠ rho_-1) can be corrected with unbiased or weighted surrogate losses. Expanded in JMLR 2018 as "Cost-Sensitive Learning with Noisy Labels." *Relation:* our alpha (FPR) and beta (FNR) are exactly these class-conditional rates. The difference is that we train a generative policy on the positives only, never a classifier.
- **Scott, Blanchard, Handy. "Classification with Asymmetric Label Noise: Consistency and Maximal Denoising." COLT 2013 (PMLR 30).** Gives consistency results when noise rates are unknown and differ between classes, assuming the true class distributions are mutually irreducible. *Relation:* it is the standard citation for "asymmetric" noise. The two error types matter differently in our setting.
- **Elkan, Noto. "Learning Classifiers from Only Positive and Unlabeled Data." KDD 2008.** If labeled positives are a uniform random sample of all positives ("selected completely at random"), dropping positives only rescales the classifier. *Relation:* this is why false negatives cost little for us. If misses are uncorrelated with the trajectory, the kept set is still an unbiased sample of successes, only smaller. A false positive instead contaminates the sample. Caveat: grader misses may correlate with trajectory style, which breaks that assumption.

## 3. Reward-model errors, over-optimization, imperfect verifiers

- **Gao, Schulman, Hilton. "Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization." ICML 2023 (PMLR 202).** Gold reward first rises, then falls as the policy optimizes a proxy RM (under both best-of-n and RL), with smooth scaling in RM size. *Relation:* filtered BC is roughly best-of-n distillation against a grader. Our false-positive harm is the classification-level version of proxy/gold divergence.
- **Skalse, Howe, Krasheninnikov, Krueger. "Defining and Characterizing Reward Gaming." NeurIPS 2022 (arXiv title: "...Reward Hacking").** Formalizes hacking as raising the proxy return while lowering the true return, and shows unhackable proxies are very restrictive. *Relation:* conceptual citation. Over self-improvement rounds, the policy may drift toward failures the grader accepts.
- **Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan. "Inference Scaling fLaws: The Limits of LLM Resampling with Imperfect Verifiers." arXiv:2411.17501, v1 Nov 2024.** v3 (Mar 2026) is retitled "The Limits of Inference Scaling Through Resampling." An ICLR 2026 poster page lists it under the new title. I could not find an official acceptance notice, so cite it as ICLR 2026 only after checking OpenReview. *False-positive argument:* when a verifier sometimes accepts wrong answers, resampling until one passes cannot push the false-positive rate down, so accuracy hits a ceiling that no amount of compute removes. Weaker models have higher false-positive rates, so they cannot catch up by resampling, and the best number of samples is often under 10. *Relation:* we make the same precision-ceiling argument for training data instead of inference outputs, in robotics.
- **Feng, Dohmatob, Yang, Charton, Kempe. "Beyond Model Collapse: Scaling Up with Synthesized Data Requires Verification." ICLR 2025.** Retraining on synthetic data avoids collapse, and can beat the generator, when a verifier or pruner selects the data, even an imperfect one. *Relation:* the closest theory to our loop, but analyzed with Gaussian mixtures and linear models.
- Very recent, unrefereed, optional: Zhou, Jiang, Wu, Zhou, "The Verifier is the Curriculum: Precision Sets the Return on Search in Code Self-Distillation," arXiv:2607.09709 (2026). They hold the admitted count fixed and find gains track filter precision, while "recall is nearly free." This is nearly our claim, for code. Reviewers may know it, so cite it and set ours apart (robot VLA, learned grader).

## Is the rho / precision framing standard?

Yes, at the level of the algebra. rho = alpha(1-q) / (alpha(1-q) + (1-beta)q) is Bayes' rule for 1 − PPV (positive predictive value) at prevalence q. This is textbook in diagnostic testing and in base-rate-fallacy discussions. **Present it as a standard identity applied to our setting, not as a new derivation.** The application, "share of failures in the imitation set = 1 − grader precision at base success rate q," appears informally in the imperfect-verifier literature (Stroebl et al.; Zhou et al. 2026 use "verifier precision" as the causal variable). I found no paper that states the closed form for filtered BC in robotics, but my search was not exhaustive.
